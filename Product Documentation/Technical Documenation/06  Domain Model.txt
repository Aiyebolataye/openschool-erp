06 — Domain Model
1. Purpose

The Domain Model defines the core business domains, entities, ownership, and relationships used throughout the Educational Institution ERP Platform.

It serves as the foundation for database design, API design, backend development, and business logic.

2. Design Principles

The domain model follows these principles:

Multi-tenant by design.
Every business record belongs to a single institution unless explicitly platform-owned.
Business domains are modular and loosely coupled.
Configuration replaces institution-specific hardcoding wherever practical.
Users, roles, and permissions are managed independently of business entities.
Relationships should reflect real-world educational operations.
Shared platform services are reused across all domains.

6. Core Relationships
Organization
One Organization manages many Institutions.
Institution
One Institution owns all institution-specific data.
One Institution may operate multiple Campuses.
People
One Student may have multiple Parents or Guardians.
One Parent may be linked to multiple Students.
One Staff member may hold multiple Roles.
One User account may be linked to one or more roles.
Academics
One Academic Session contains multiple Terms.
One Term contains multiple Classes.
One Class contains multiple Students.
One Subject may be taught by multiple Teachers.
One Teacher may teach multiple Subjects.
Students enroll in multiple Subjects.
Subjects belong to one or more Departments or Programs.
Learning
One Subject contains multiple Lesson Notes.
One Lesson Note may contain multiple Learning Resources.
One Assignment belongs to one Subject.
One Assignment has many Student Submissions.
Finance
One Student may have multiple Invoices.
One Invoice may contain multiple Payment Transactions.
One Fee Structure may apply to multiple Students.
Student Success
One Student may have multiple Welfare Cases.
One Student may have multiple Counselling Sessions.
One Student owns one Portfolio containing multiple achievements and records.
Platform Services
Every Request may generate one or more Approval records.
Every significant action creates an Audit Log.
Notifications may be triggered by any module through the Automation Framework.
7. Global Business Rules
Every institution-specific record belongs to exactly one institution.
Platform-owned entities are shared across institutions where applicable.
Users may hold multiple roles.
Permissions are determined by assigned roles and permission groups.
Institution data must remain isolated from other institutions.
Audit logs are immutable.
Soft deletion should be used where recovery is required.
Entity relationships must preserve referential integrity.
Business rules should be enforced within the domain layer rather than duplicated across modules.
8. Domain Principles
Domains communicate through shared platform services rather than direct dependencies wherever possible.
Business logic belongs to its owning domain.
Modules should remain independent while sharing common services such as Authentication, Notifications, Automation, Reporting and Audit Logging.
New modules should extend existing domains where appropriate before introducing new domains.
Domain models should remain technology-agnostic and independent of database or framework implementations.
